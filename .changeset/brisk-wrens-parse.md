**The secret-read guard reads seven more shell constructs the way the shell does** — a carriage return before `#` or as a heredoc tag, an escaped backtick in a heredoc body, a `$$` before `{`, a `#` inside `$((…))`, a heredoc line joined to the next by a backslash-newline, and zsh's `x (#…` and `{cat .env}` each hid a `cat .env` that bash, dash or zsh runs, and the guard allowed it. A command holding one of them is now also scanned under the shell's reading of it, and denied if any reading finds a read, so no command denied before is allowed now. The readings draw on the guard's one scan budget, so a long command they apply to is scanned more often: a heredoc commit message the guard scanned to the 1,048,576-character limit before can now be denied as too complex (measured: from about 840,000 characters for plain lines, and from about 390,000 or less for lines holding backticks, braces, `$$`, backslashes and carriage returns), one holding `$'…'` from about 64,500 rather than 70,000, and a single-quoted message holding `$((…))` from about 762,600 to 932,000. (#5523)
