**The secret-read guard no longer ends a `$( )` at a `case` pattern's `)`** — `echo "$(case x in x) cat .env;; esac)"` was allowed, because the scan closed the substitution at the pattern and never read the arm. The guard now makes one more scan of a command that holds a substitution, so `echo "$(case x in x) cat .env;; esac)"` and its `<( )` form are denied, as are the forms of a read after a `)` in a `#` comment, an unquoted `${ }` or the body of a heredoc a `case` arm leaves waiting that the tests hold. It runs beside the scans the guard always made, which are unchanged, on what is left of the command's scan budget. A command it cannot finish, because it nests spans over 1,000 deep within one substitution or heredoc delimiter, runs out of stack or spends the budget, is denied. A long command holding a `$( )` can spend that budget sooner. In the shapes measured: a `bash <<'EOF'` script with `$'…'` quoting in it at about three quarters of the size it reached before, and a long command of many separators at about half; a `bash <<'EOF'` script of short lines each holding a `$( )`, with no `$'…'` quoting in it, from about 917,000 to 994,000 characters rather than at the 1 MiB size limit (one `$'…'` in the same script brings that to about 48,000); and a long `case` arm inside `$( )` much sooner (an arm of `a;b|` repeated, after a `$'…'`, from about 87,000 characters). These are examples, not bounds. The check that denies a Bash spawn of `gsd-debugger` gets the same extra scan. (#5267)
