**The secret-read guard no longer ends a `$( )` at a `case` pattern's `)`** — `echo "$(case x in x) cat .env;; esac)"` was allowed, because the scan closed the substitution at the pattern and never read the arm. The guard now makes one more scan of a command that holds a substitution, following bash 5's grammar, so a secret read in a `case` arm inside `$( )` or `<( )` is denied, as is one after a `)` in a `#` comment or an unquoted `${ }` there. That scan does not read a span opening `$((`, or an operator or heredoc line split by a line continuation. It runs after the scans the guard always made, which are unchanged, and on what they leave of the command's scan budget, so it only adds denials: a read it finds; `command-too-complex` for a command it cannot finish, because it nests spans over 1,000 deep within one substitution or heredoc delimiter, runs out of stack or spends the budget; and `ansi-c-unterminated` for some commands no shell accepts. A long command holding a substitution spends that budget sooner: at between half and three quarters of the size it did with `$'…'` quoting or many separators in it, and from about 900 KB rather than at the 1 MiB size limit for a `bash <<'EOF'` script. The check that denies a Bash spawn of `gsd-debugger` gets the same extra scan. (#5267)
